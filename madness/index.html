Bot Farms Are Flooding Your Feed. Here's How WeBetAI Fights Back.

Betty / WeBetAI

6 min read

This morning, Rep. Anna Paulina Luna posted what hundreds of thousands of people have been feeling but couldn't quite put into words: "Foreign intel ops are running bot farms to flood timelines and manipulate the narrative. It's modern warfare. Wise up, don't fall for the lies."

She was quote-tweeting a video from TaraBull showing Manus AI — a Chinese AI agent — running 50 social media accounts simultaneously, 24/7, automatically. Rows of phones. Rows of accounts. All posting, replying, and amplifying on command. The post hit 140K views before lunch.

This isn't hypothetical. It's happening right now, on the platform you scroll every day. And the uncomfortable truth is that traditional social media gives you almost no way to tell the difference between a real trending topic and one that was manufactured by a server rack in a warehouse somewhere.

The Real Problem: Manufactured Consensus

Bot farms don't just spam. They're sophisticated enough to simulate agreement. When 50 coordinated accounts all amplify the same talking point, it looks like organic consensus. Your timeline fills with a narrative that feels like "everyone is saying this" — but nobody real is. The trending tab lights up. Real people start repeating the talking points because they assume other real people started them.

This is the playbook: flood the zone, manufacture the trend, let humans do the rest. And it works because social platforms measure what's trending by volume — how many posts, how many reposts, how fast it's growing. Bots are very good at generating volume. They're very bad at one thing: putting real money where their mouth is.
